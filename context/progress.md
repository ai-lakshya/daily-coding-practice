| 2026-09-16 | contest | ct-01 | AtCoder Beginner Contest 475 — binary search follow-up (§2.4) | | | Follow-up study session on C's binary search, written up as CONTEST.md §2.4. The enabling step for `lower_bound`/`upper_bound` is algebraic — isolate the element on the left (`pre[curr] - pre[minLeft] <= budget` -> `pre[curr] <= pre[minLeft] + budget`), which also moves the overflow risk to the right-hand side. Both functions return a *first*; "last" forms are that minus one. `std::partition_point` is the general form (`lower_bound`/`upper_bound` are it with the predicate baked in) and is the one that transfers to "binary search the answer", where there is no array to search — its law is that the predicate must run true-then-false, so a backwards predicate gets negated and the answer moves from `pp - 1` to `pp`. Two findings specific to `c2.cpp`: `post` is entirely redundant with `pre` (`post[i] - post[j] == pre[j] - pre[i]`), which also removes the undefined-behaviour risk of binary-searching a descending range; and the `left == minLeft` / `max(startPos, ...)` guard was an artefact of searching a window wider than the problem allows, not a real edge case — constraining `[first, last)` makes the `-1` provably in-bounds. Also learned the hard way while drafting: the two cost accountings (charge `2*dist(S,i)` up front vs. one trip out plus the full walk back) are not interchangeable mid-loop — mixing them triple-charges `dist(S,i)`, caught by stress-testing against an O(n^2) brute force rather than by reading the code. `c2.cpp` left untouched as the historical record |
